DIY Steps You Can Take Right Now

Start by turning off power to the furnace and replacing the air filter if it looks gray or clogged. Check that supply vents are open and free of furniture or rugs. Inspect the thermostat batteries and confirm the setting switches from cool to heat. Listen for unusual rattling or clicking when the system tries to start. These basic checks can restore function in many cases, but stop immediately if you smell gas or see soot around the unit.

When Professional Furnace Repair in Montclair Is Required

Call a licensed technician for any gas odor, repeated short cycling, or lack of heat after filter changes. Carbon monoxide testing, heat exchanger inspection, and electrical component checks require specialized tools and safety training. In Montclair and nearby Ontario, technicians also verify proper venting because Santa Ana winds can affect exhaust flow on rooftop units. Never attempt gas line work or combustion adjustments yourself.

What to Expect During a Service Visit

A thorough inspection includes measuring temperature rise across the heat exchanger, testing safety switches, cleaning the blower assembly, and checking duct connections for leaks. Technicians will also review the age and condition of the unit to discuss realistic repair versus replacement options. Expect clear explanations of findings without pressure tactics, along with written recommendations tailored to your home's setup.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I change my furnace filter in Montclair?

Every 60-90 days during winter, or sooner if you notice heavy dust from Santa Ana winds.

Is it safe to run a furnace that makes clicking noises?

No. Shut the system off and schedule service, as clicking can indicate ignition or fan motor problems.

Do I need a permit for furnace repair in Montclair?

Major repairs or replacements usually require permits; a licensed contractor handles this for you.

Can I repair my furnace myself if the filter change didn't help?

Only basic filter and thermostat checks are safe for DIY. Gas and electrical work must be left to professionals.
