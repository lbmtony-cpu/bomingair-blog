Common Problems Seen in Upland Commercial Systems

Most calls for commercial HVAC service in Upland involve restricted airflow from packed filters or dirty evaporator coils. Santa Ana winds push fine dust through intake screens, and without regular attention the system works harder. Thermostats placed near south-facing windows can also misread room temperature after a heat wave.

Another frequent issue is condensate drain clogs caused by mineral buildup in hard water areas. When drains back up, water can damage ceiling tiles or trigger safety switches that shut the unit down during peak afternoon heat. In Ontario and Claremont, we see similar patterns tied to the same regional weather.

Simple Steps Owners Can Handle Themselves

Start by checking and replacing pleated filters every 30 to 45 days during summer. Keep at least a month’s supply on site so no one skips the task. Walk the roof or ground-level units monthly and note any unusual rattling or visible damage to insulation on refrigerant lines.

Clear at least two feet of space around outdoor condensers so airflow stays unobstructed. Trim nearby landscaping and remove leaves or paper that wind has deposited. These basic actions reduce the load on the system and help spot problems early.

When It Is Time to Call a Professional

Leave refrigerant checks, electrical diagnostics, and belt tension adjustments to licensed technicians. Attempting these without proper gauges or training can void warranties and create safety risks. If a unit short-cycles, blows warm air, or shows ice on the coils, stop using it and schedule commercial HVAC service in Upland right away.

Technicians also inspect economizers, verify static pressure, and test safety controls that protect against overheating or gas leaks in heating season. These steps require specialized tools and knowledge of current energy codes.

Planning Ahead for Year-Round Reliability

Schedule two maintenance visits per year: one before summer and one before the first cold snap. Keep records of filter changes and past repairs so patterns become clear over time. Many Upland property managers also budget for capacitor and contactor replacements every three to five years because heat shortens their life.

Ask the technician to review ductwork access points and insulation condition while on site. Small leaks in return air paths can pull in attic dust and raise indoor allergen levels. Addressing these during routine service prevents larger issues later.

If your building has multiple rooftop units, label them clearly and note which zones they serve. This simple step speeds up future service calls and helps new staff understand the system layout. Consistent care keeps energy costs predictable even when temperatures climb past 100 degrees.
